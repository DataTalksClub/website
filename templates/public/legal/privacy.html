2. Data we handle

Website and public content

Public pages include GitHub-authored and versioned articles, podcasts, books, documentation, wiki material, courses, events, and public editorial people records. Source revision and validation data support publication. A public editorial person is separate from a private member profile and is never linked by inference.

Accounts, sign-in, and member profiles

Account records can include email identity, authentication-provider links, security and session state, preferences, and audit evidence. Private member profiles can include the fields a member enters for community and learner onboarding, such as display or certificate name, country or region, organization, work status, professional role, and seniority.

Slack onboarding

The website records profile completion and eligibility for the shared DataTalks.Club Slack access link. It does not infer Slack membership, operate a Slack invitation API, synchronize a public member directory, or make the private profile public.

Courses and cohorts

Course records can include enrollment and registration data, a deliberately limited snapshot of confirmed profile fields, the selected course or cohort, comments, privacy-notice and separate optional marketing-consent evidence, homework and project links, answers, scores, peer reviews, complaints, leaderboard results, and certificates. Profile changes apply to future registrations unless a specific correction process says otherwise.

Events and registrations

Event records can include the event, email, name or other fields requested by the registration flow, attendance or status, the accepted privacy notice, and separate optional marketing consent. Verification and management tokens are stored and handled as protected credentials, not as analytics or public content.

Transactional email

The website sends transactional email through Relay, the one service it uses for email delivery. It keeps only the record of what was to be sent, the redacted delivery status, and the reconciliation and audit data needed for that purpose. The sending provider sits behind Relay, so the website itself holds no second email integration.

Country suggestion

On some onboarding pages, an AWS CloudFront country header may be used only to suggest an editable country value. The member confirms or changes it. The header is not retained or logged as a separate fact about the member.

Security and operational data

The service handles bounded request and correlation identifiers, safe path and response metadata, security events, audit records, job state, and error diagnostics. Logs exclude authorization data, cookies, complete query strings, raw IP addresses, profile values, registration answers, Slack secrets, and message bodies under the documented application and infrastructure policy.

3. Why we use data and lawful bases

  • to provide an account, community access, events, courses, submissions, reviews, and certificates;
  • to perform requested services and manage the relationship with participants;
  • to secure, diagnose, audit, and maintain the website and its integrations;
  • to publish content or contributions when the contributor has authorized publication;
  • to comply with legal duties and establish, exercise, or defend legal claims; and
  • to use optional analytics or marketing data only when a separate valid consent applies.

Depending on the activity and applicable law, these purposes rely on performance of a requested service, legitimate interests in operating a safe educational community, consent, or legal obligations. Consent can be withdrawn for future processing without changing the lawfulness of earlier processing.

4. Cookies and analytics

Necessary session and CSRF cookies protect sign-in and form submissions. A minimal dtc_analytics_consent cookie stores version 1 of the allow or deny choice for 180 days using a site-wide path, SameSite=Lax, and Secure on HTTPS. It contains no account, profile, country, or session identifier.

Optional analytics fail closed. The website loads no analytics provider, carries no measurement ID or event taxonomy, and contacts no analytics endpoint. If optional measurement is added later, it may start only after an affirmative choice and must not receive an email, profile value, country signal, raw IP, account or database ID, session or CSRF value, credential, Slack secret, or other direct identifier.

Use the Analytics preferences button in any footer to allow, reject, or withdraw the choice. Rejection and withdrawal expire optional first-party analytics cookies and do not block site, signup, Slack, course, or event functionality.

5. Recipients, subprocessors, and transfers

Data is shared only as needed with the services that operate the requested function. Current boundaries include AWS hosting, database, storage, edge delivery, and operational logging; GitHub for public source content and a chosen sign-in method; Google for a chosen sign-in method; Slack when a member follows the community access link; and Relay for transactional email. Course, event, and public-content destinations may use their own linked services.

These services may process data outside the country where a participant lives. For the regions and transfer safeguards that apply to a particular service, write to alexey@datatalks.club. No optional analytics processor is configured.

6. Retention

Records are kept only as long as they are needed, on the following schedule:

  • unverified course or event registrations are removed after 14 days;
  • event registration personal data is removed or anonymized 90 days after the event;
  • course enrollment, submissions, grading, certificates, and consent evidence remain while the educational record is active, then follow the published deletion or anonymization schedule;
  • Relay email records and redacted delivery metadata are retained for 180 days;
  • security audit evidence is retained for one year; and
  • operational logs are retained for 30 days.

Account and profile data is retained while the account is active and then deleted or anonymized subject to legal, security, abuse-prevention, and educational-record requirements.

7. Your choices and rights

Depending on applicable law, you may ask for access, export, correction, deletion, restriction, or portability of personal data, and may object to processing based on legitimate interests. You may withdraw analytics or marketing consent for future processing at any time. You may also complain to the competent data protection authority.

Some public GitHub-authored material is corrected in the repository it is written in, while private account, profile, course, and event records use the relevant account or support process. Identity and authorization may be checked before fulfilling a request.

8. Security

DataTalks.Club uses access control, secure cookies, request forgery protection, encryption in transit, bounded audit records, redaction, least-privilege service roles, protected credentials, and reviewed deployment controls. No internet service can promise absolute security.

9. Minors

DataTalks.Club is an educational community, not a service designed to profile children. A person who cannot validly agree to these terms where they live should use the service only with appropriate parent or guardian involvement. If you believe a child has given us personal data without that involvement, write to alexey@datatalks.club and we will remove it.

10. Changes and contact

A material change to this policy is published here with a new last-updated date. For a privacy question, a request about your data, or a complaint, write to alexey@datatalks.club; the operator's postal details are in the Impressum.
