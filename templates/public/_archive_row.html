{% load people accessibility %} {% comment %} One row of an archive: a date on the left, and the thing itself as a card. Every dated index on the site draws the same row — the blog and the books archive, the podcast index, and each band of contributions on a person's profile — and until this partial existed each of them drew it again in its own template. The row had been converged by hand four times in one session (drop the "By"/"With" label, shrink the face, move the credit to the foot of the card, put the date on the left in two lines), and every one of those changes had to be made in four files. This is the one place to make the fifth. Call it once per record, passing only the parts that record actually has: {% include "public/_archive_row.html" with row_title=record.title row_url=record.public_path row_date=record.published only %} The call is one long line because a Django tag cannot be broken across lines. The slots, all optional except the title and the link: * `row_date` — anything a publication date parses out of. It is drawn as the fixed-width rail, day above year, so every rail down a page is the same height. A record with no date gives its rail back to the card rather than leaving a column of empty space (seventeen podcast episodes have none). * `row_mark` — `"play"` draws the podcast disc as the card's leading mark. * `row_eyebrow` — one mono line above the title: what this row is to the reader of this page ("guest", "speaker", "author"). * `row_pill`, `row_pill_variant`, `row_pill_extra` — the status pill and, when a list mixes past and future work, the second bare pill that says which. A podcast row uses `row_pill` as its blue season/episode eyebrow instead. * `row_summary` — clamped, because the record's own page carries the rest. * `row_credits` — the people who made it, resolved through `person_chip`, at the foot of the card and with no "By": a face beside a name under a title is already read as the person who made it. * `row_class`, `row_hook` — a class and one bare `data-` attribute for the surface's own hooks. Every caller passes `only`, and `content/tests/test_archive_row.py` holds them to it: the row draws what it was given and never something the page around it happened to have in scope. What it deliberately does not do is the events index (`templates/public/ events.html`). That row's rail carries three facts rather than a publication date — the day, the weekday and the start clock — and its card holds the kind pill, which takes focus for its explanation, above a link stretched over the whole card. Expressing either here would have meant a flag per difference. The events card's own shared rules live in `templates/core/_design_system.html` instead, so the homepage and the events index still draw one card. {% endcomment %} {% with row_rail=row_date|yesno:"archive-row,archive-row archive-row-undated" %}
